Gran Torino (2008) · Clint Eastwood

Is the American Dream for everyone?

A look at Walt, Thao and the question of who gets a fair chance

By Lut, Tobias, Erik & Luis

The statement

“The American Dream is not equally accessible to everyone. Your background determines your chances of success.”

The American Dream: if you work hard, you can be successful in the USA – no matter where you come from.

Question: Does Gran Torino show that this is true – or not?

Walt Kowalski

Walt's life and his idea of success

  • Korean War veteran, worked 50 years at Ford
  • Has a house, a lawn and his 1972 Gran Torino
  • For him, success = hard work, owning things, being independent
  • He is a white American – the system was open for him
  • But: he is lonely and his sons don't care about him
Walt reached the “old” American Dream – but he is not happy.
Thao Vang Lor

Thao's situation and his chances

  • Young Hmong American, lives in a poor area of Detroit
  • No father, no male role model, no money
  • Gang pressure – his cousin Spider wants him to join
  • Shy, has no plan for his future at first
  • Walt helps him: work, tools, a job in construction
Thao has talent, but he needs someone who opens doors for him.
Education, work and family

What helps people to move up?

  • Work: Thao learns to fix things and gets a real job
  • Education: Sue goes to college – she has better chances
  • Family: the Hmong family is close and supports each other
  • Walt's own family is broken, even though they have money
  • Contacts matter: without Walt, Thao gets no job
Hard work alone is not enough – you also need help and connections.
Prejudice and racism

Barriers because of background

  • Walt uses racist words against his Hmong neighbours
  • Sue is attacked by a group of Black men – just for who she is
  • Gangs control the streets – violence instead of chances
  • Poor neighbourhood = bad schools, few jobs
  • Walt changes: he sees Thao and Sue as people, not as “foreigners”
Prejudice closes doors – but people can change.
Money vs. happiness

Is material success everything?

  • Walt's sons have money, cars and nice houses – but no real relationships
  • The Hmong family has little money – but a strong community
  • Walt finds happiness through friendship with Thao and Sue
  • At the end he gives Thao his most valued thing: the Gran Torino
Real success = respect, friendship and doing the right thing.
Conclusion

We partly agree with the statement

YES, because …

  • Thao starts with fewer chances than Walt
  • Racism, poverty and gangs block his way
  • Without help he would probably fail

BUT …

  • Background is not everything
  • Work, education and support can change a life
  • Thao gets a real chance at the end – the car is a symbol of that

“The American Dream is possible – but not everyone starts at the same line.”
